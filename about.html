Hi there — I'm Ajeet.

A product designer who has spent the past four-plus years building for both B2B and B2C, at Juspay and Porter. Right now I'm solving retention and enterprise products at Porter.

I got into design by accident.

I'm a computer science grad who stumbled into design sideways — making event posters and building web frontends in college. I'd sketch interface ideas on paper, look at what I'd drawn, and keep landing on the same thought.

There's got to be a better way to do this.

Me, every time, in college

That thought is what led me to product design. I got fascinated by the idea that behind every feature I used day to day, someone had put that much thought and that many iterations into it. I wanted to be the person doing that — and here I am.

I'm usually mid-experiment.

Always curious, always pulling apart some new tool. Beyond product design itself I tinker with motion design and advanced prototypes — the kind that are close enough to the real thing that nobody has to imagine it.

At Porter I built a near-app prototype of our redesigned customer app, real enough to put in front of users for testing and to get product and engineering aligned on the same thing rather than three different mental models.

Three habits that survived every team I've worked on.

Iteration with intent

Volume isn't the point. Every round should be answering a question I couldn't answer in the last one — otherwise it's just redecorating.

Sketch ideas, prototype realities

Paper is for thinking fast and being wrong cheaply. The prototype is where an idea has to survive contact with how it actually behaves.

Prototypes are bridges between ideas and experiences

A good prototype ends the argument. Everyone stops describing what they think the thing is and just looks at it together.
